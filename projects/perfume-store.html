The problem

Concurrent carts and live inventory don't forgive a sloppy schema.

An e-commerce catalog with real-time stock control needs referential integrity enforced at the database layer, not patched in application code. The design goal was zero redundancy and no update anomalies — even under concurrent checkout transactions.

Data model

Five entity groups, normalized to third normal form.

1Users & auth

Registration, login, and session handling with hashed passwords.

  • users, sessions
  • role-based admin flag
2Catalog

Brand-partitioned product data with shared attributes.

  • perfumes, brands
  • pricing & imagery
3Orders & cart

Transactional tables guarded by triggers and ACID commits.

  • cart_items, orders, order_details
  • stock decrement on checkout

Security & integrity

Enforced at the middleware and database layer, not just the UI.

01

Middleware route protection

Restricted pages — checkout, admin — check authentication state server-side before rendering, not just hiding a button client-side.

02

PL/SQL triggers & ACID transactions

Stock control and order writes are wrapped in transactions with automated triggers, so a failed checkout can't leave inventory half-updated.
