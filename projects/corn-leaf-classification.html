Case study — explainable AI & medical-adjacent imaging

A model that points at the lesion, not just the leaf.

Early diagnosis of corn leaf disease protects crop yield, but a farmer's phone can't run a heavy vision transformer in the field. This project distills a lesion-aware teacher into a MobileNetV3 student small enough for edge inference — and Grad-CAM confirms it's actually looking at the diseased tissue, not the background.

The problem

Accuracy alone isn't enough for a diagnostic tool.

A classifier can be accurate for the wrong reasons — keying off background soil color instead of the lesion itself. And a 97%-accurate teacher model is useless if it can't run on the low-power hardware available in the field. The project had to solve interpretability, compression, and calibration together.

01

Lesion-Aware Inception Classifier (LAIC)

A dual-branch teacher: one branch reads the raw image, the other reads a U-Net-masked version with the background attenuated — forcing the network to weight pathology over context.

02

Multi-teacher knowledge distillation

LAIC (Inception V3 + U-Net) and a DeiT-Small transformer both teach a MobileNetV3 student, combining logit distillation, attention transfer, and GAP feature matching into one compact model.

03

Quantization-aware training to INT8

The student is trained with quantization in the loop, not applied after the fact — preserving accuracy while cutting CPU inference latency to ~15.5–17ms on a 299×299 image.

04

Grad-CAM verification + selective abstention

Grad-CAM confirms the model's highest activations land on diseased tissue. Temperature scaling and selective prediction let the model abstain on uncertain cases instead of guessing — reaching 100% precision at ~71% coverage.
